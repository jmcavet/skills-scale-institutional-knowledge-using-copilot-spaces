# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the core project management process guidance used by OctoAcme. It provides a lightweight, repeatable framework for initiating, planning, executing, releasing, and improving cross-functional work.

## Overview

OctoAcme follows a practical project lifecycle designed to keep work aligned to customer value, team capacity, and measurable outcomes:

1. Initiation — confirm the problem, stakeholders, success metrics, and go/no-go decision.
2. Planning — define scope, backlog, estimates, dependencies, and milestones.
3. Execution & Tracking — run the team rhythm, review progress, manage blockers, and keep delivery visible.
4. Risk & Communication — identify risks, escalate appropriately, and keep stakeholders informed.
5. Release & Deployment — validate readiness, roll out changes, and verify impact.
6. Retrospective & Improvement — capture learning and turn it into action.

## Project management process summary

### 1. Initiation
The project starts with a clear business need, measurable outcome, and stakeholder alignment. Teams create a one-pager with the problem statement, goals, success metrics, assumptions, risks, and a proposed team.

### 2. Planning
Approved initiatives are converted into an actionable plan with a prioritized backlog, milestone map, estimates, dependencies, and a definition of done. This step ensures the work is scoped appropriately and ready for execution.

### 3. Execution and tracking
The team works in a consistent rhythm with standups, weekly delivery syncs, reviews, and visible tracking on the project board. Progress is measured against milestones, and blockers are escalated through defined levels.

### 4. Risk and communication
Risks, dependencies, and stakeholder needs are tracked and communicated regularly. Clear ownership and escalation paths help teams surface issues early and keep decision-makers informed.

### 5. Release and deployment
Changes are only released after acceptance criteria are met, CI and security checks pass, and rollout plans are documented. Teams prepare release notes, smoke tests, and rollback procedures to reduce production risk.

### 6. Retrospective and continuous improvement
At the end of a sprint, milestone, or release, the team captures what went well, what needs work, and which actions should be tracked. These improvements are fed back into the backlog as ongoing process improvements.
